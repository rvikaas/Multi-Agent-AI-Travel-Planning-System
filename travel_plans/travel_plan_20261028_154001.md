## PRACTICAL TIPS TO KEEP THE BUDGET IN CHECK  

| Tip | Why it helps |
|-----|--------------|
| **Book train tickets a day early** on the IRCTC website or at the station – second‑class is cheapest and seats are plentiful on the Delhi‑Agra route. | Avoid last‑minute price hikes or the need for a more expensive AC ticket. |
| **Stay in mixed‑gender dorms** (women‑only rooms are a bit pricier). Most hostels provide free Wi‑Fi, lockers, and a communal kitchen if you want to cook a simple meal. | Saves $2‑$4 per night vs. private rooms. |
| **Eat where locals eat** – street stalls, dhabas, and “canteens” serve filling meals for $1‑$3. Look for places with a line; it’s a good sign of hygiene and flavor. | Keeps food cost under $8 per day. |
| **Use metro & auto‑rickshaws** instead of app‑based cabs. Carry small change (₹10, ₹20, ₹50) for auto fares. | Metro fares in Delhi are $0.20‑$0.50 per ride; auto‑rickshaws are $1‑$2 for short hops. |
| **Carry a reusable water bottle** and refill at hostels or filtered water stations (₹10 per refill). | Avoid buying bottled water ($0.30‑$0.50 each). |
| **Travel light** – a 20‑L backpack fits all essentials and avoids extra luggage fees on trains. | Keeps you mobile and reduces risk of lost bags. |
| **Negotiate entry fees** (some smaller monuments have “donation” boxes). If you’re a student, bring your ID – many sites offer a 10‑15 % discount. | Saves a few dollars per site. |
